Overview

About the thesis

This Master's thesis investigates torque vectoring for a four-wheel-drive battery electric truck using an e-axle configuration.

The work focuses on how the available electric powertrain configuration can be used to distribute torque between the driven wheels and improve the vehicle's dynamic behaviour.

The thesis was conducted as part of the Master's programme in Systems, Controls and Mechatronics at Chalmers University of Technology.

Technical focus

Torque vectoring & vehicle dynamics

The project examines the use of independently controllable electric machines to influence the distribution of torque between the wheels of the vehicle.

By controlling the torque delivered by the individual electric motors, the vehicle can generate additional yaw moments and influence its handling characteristics.

The work combines electric powertrain modelling, vehicle dynamics and control to investigate the potential of torque vectoring in a heavy-duty battery electric vehicle.
